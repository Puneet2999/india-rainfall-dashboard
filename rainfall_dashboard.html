Analytical Caveat & Climatological Limits

Why is univariate $R^2$ low? Indian monsoon annual precipitation exhibits minimal serial autocorrelation ($\phi \approx 0.0$ to $0.2$). Precipitation variance is driven primarily by planetary ocean-atmosphere coupled oscillations:

  • ENSO (El Niño-Southern Oscillation)
  • IOD (Indian Ocean Dipole)
  • MJO (Madden-Julian Oscillation)

Because these teleconnections operate on non-linear physical cycles rather than univariate autoregression, simple linear extrapolation cannot reliably forecast extreme drought or flood swings.

Model Conclusion: The 30-year IMD Climatological Normal baseline achieves comparable or superior test RMSE to linear regression in most subdivisions, serving as the most defensible baseline forecast for 2016.
*Forecast is strictly for historical verification year 2016 (following 2015 historical endpoint), not a 2027 projection.

Evidence-Based Climatological Insights & Dataset Validation

Synthesized statistical findings validated against the 1901–2015 historical time-series and district climatological normals

1. Extreme Seasonal Concentration

75.3% of Rainfall in 122 Days

Nationwide, 1,062.3 mm out of 1,411.0 mm falls within the four-month June–September Southwest Monsoon window. However, the peninsular southeast (Tamil Nadu) deviates dramatically, receiving 47.7% of its rainfall in the Oct–Dec Northeast Monsoon season due to retreating monsoonal troughs over the Bay of Bengal.

SW Monsoon Share: 75.29% | NE: 10.87%
2. Orographic Spatial Gradient

11.7-Fold Geographic Contrast

India exhibits massive orographic precipitation gradients: Western Ghats windward coastal tracts (Coastal Karnataka at 3,408.4 mm, Konkan & Goa at 2,977.7 mm) and northeastern Himalayan foothills (Arunachal Pradesh at 3,418.9 mm) receive over an order of magnitude more rain than the rain-shadow Thar desert (West Rajasthan at 292.7 mm).

Max: 3,418.9 mm vs Min: 292.7 mm
3. Pan-Indian Drought Clustering

Severe Deficits Clustered by ENSO

Historical all-India drought years (1918 at -24.9%, 1972 at -18.7%, 1965 at -17.5%, 1987 at -14.8%, 2002 at -16.1%, 2014 at -10.4%, 2015 at -9.2%) correlate closely with strong El Niño phases in the equatorial Pacific, demonstrating that multi-year volatility is climate-cycle driven rather than random walk.

Driest Historical Year: 1972 (1,146.5 mm)
4. Data Validation & Integrity

Rigorous Missingness Handling

Exactly 26 annual records and 63 monthly values were missing in the historical series, isolated to 6 island/remote subdivisions (Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar, Arunachal Pradesh). Zero-replacement was strictly avoided (which would falsely simulate absolute drought); instead, climatological monthly median imputation was applied, preserving true series fidelity.
